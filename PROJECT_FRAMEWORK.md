# Mustanef Project Framework

## 1. Purpose

Mustanef strengthens preparedness for transformative artificial intelligence in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). It connects research, policy, strategic foresight, public dialogue, partnerships, and accessible learning to help societies anticipate and shape profound AI-driven change.

## 2. The Challenge

Many LMICs face a dual risk: being excluded from the benefits of advanced AI while remaining highly exposed to its economic, educational, health, governance, and social consequences. Readiness cannot be limited to technical adoption. It must include institutional capacity, public understanding, inclusion, accountability, local knowledge, and long-term strategic planning.

## 3. Overall Goal

To develop an adaptable, evidence-informed framework that helps LMIC stakeholders assess transformative-AI readiness, anticipate major transitions, strengthen local agency, and design inclusive policy and community responses.

## 4. Strategic Objectives

1. Examine plausible transformative-AI scenarios and their implications for LMICs.
2. Assess readiness across institutions, policies, skills, infrastructure, and society.
3. Translate emerging AI developments into practical policy tools and public knowledge.
4. Strengthen meaningful dialogue among researchers, policymakers, educators, civil society, and communities.
5. Promote responsible, inclusive, and locally grounded AI preparedness.
6. Build partnerships for comparative learning and adaptation across LMIC contexts.

## 5. Integrated Workstreams

### Mustanef

The research, policy, and strategic-foresight workstream focuses on:

- readiness assessment;
- scenario development;
- comparative LMIC research;
- policy briefs and practical tools;
- institutional and research partnerships;
- strategic dialogue and knowledge exchange.

### ANAwAI — Ana wa AI | أنا والذكاء الاصطناعي

The accessible public-learning and community-engagement workstream focuses on:

- bilingual learning resources;
- public seminars and facilitated dialogues;
- community and educator engagement;
- awareness of AI opportunities, risks, rights, and responsibilities;
- community feedback that informs research and policy work.

## 6. Priority Domains

- Education and future skills
- Health and wellbeing
- Employment and economic transition
- Social protection and inequality
- Governance and public institutions
- Digital inclusion and human rights
- Community resilience and human development

## 7. General Methodology

Mustanef uses an iterative cycle:

1. **Foresight:** identify emerging capabilities, uncertainties, and plausible scenarios.
2. **Assessment:** examine readiness gaps, capacities, risks, and opportunities.
3. **Dialogue:** engage institutions, experts, and communities.
4. **Policy Translation:** develop practical briefs, frameworks, and recommendations.
5. **Public Learning:** translate findings into accessible bilingual resources.
6. **Review and Adaptation:** update assumptions and tools as AI capabilities evolve.

## 8. Proposed Outputs

Subject to partnerships and resources, proposed outputs include:

- a transformative-AI readiness framework;
- a Jordan-focused readiness assessment;
- comparative LMIC case studies;
- scenario and foresight reports;
- policy briefs and decision-support tools;
- bilingual ANAwAI learning resources;
- public dialogues and stakeholder workshops;
- an open evidence and knowledge repository.

## 9. Intended Users

- Policymakers and public institutions
- Universities and research centers
- Educators and training providers
- Civil-society organizations
- Development partners and funders
- Community leaders, youth, and the wider public

## 10. Guiding Principles

- Human agency and dignity
- Inclusion and social justice
- Responsible and accountable AI
- Local capacity and knowledge ownership
- Evidence-based policy
- Bilingual accessibility
- Community participation
- Adaptability to technological change

## 11. Geographic Approach

Jordan serves as an initial applied context for developing and testing the framework. The approach is designed for adaptation and comparative learning across other low- and middle-income countries.

## 12. Evidence and Status

Digital Al-Jazari’s public AI engagement has been active since 2025 through seminars, cultural and educational activities, institutional participation, video documentation, and media coverage. These completed activities form an early evidence base.

Future research, assessment, policy, partnership, and expansion activities are strategic directions subject to funding, institutional cooperation, and available resources.
